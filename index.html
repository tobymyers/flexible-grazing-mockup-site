How we draw the riparian zones

  1. Creek bottoms. The University of Montana's Mesic Analysis Platform (mesic.app) outlines every valley bottom in the sagebrush West (Landsat satellite, 30 m).
  2. The green test. A spot counts if it stayed green late into summer in at least 5 of the last 10 years. Greenness is measured with NDVI, from the same UM Mesic Analysis Platform's annual Landsat maps.
  3. The irrigation check. Land that is green because someone waters it is not excluded. It shows dotted, for you to decide. Irrigation comes from IrrMapper, the University of Montana's annual irrigation maps.
  4. Rivers and wetlands. Channels and wet ground are filled in from the US Fish & Wildlife Service's National Wetlands Inventory and USGS 3DHP river maps.
  5. Springs. Mapped spring points from USGS 3DHP hydrography each get a ready-made guard circle. Some points are old, so every guard can be removed.
  6. Collar limits. Anything under 10 m wide is dropped, because collars cannot hold a line that tight. Under 25 m shows faded as "narrow."
  7. Roads stay open. Road lines come from the US Census (TIGER) and state road maps. And every line here is a proposal. The rancher's edits are the final word.
